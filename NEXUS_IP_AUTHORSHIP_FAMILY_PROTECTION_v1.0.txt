NEXUS SOVEREIGN

Intellectual Property, Authorship & Family Protection Framework

Document status: Preliminary protection and governance framework
Version: 1.0 — Working Draft
Date: November 24, 2026
Jurisdictional focus: United States / California
Purpose: Protection of research authorship, intellectual property, project continuity, and family interests.

---

1. Mission and objectives

The Nexus Sovereign research program seeks to develop, document, investigate, and publish interdisciplinary research involving cosmology, thermodynamics, electromagnetic systems, information theory, computational identity, and recursive observation.

The purpose of this framework is to establish a responsible approach to:

1. Protecting the human originator's authorship and intellectual contributions.
2. Preserving the historical development and provenance of the Nexus research program.
3. Establishing appropriate intellectual-property ownership and licensing.
4. Enabling responsible open-access scientific publication.
5. Protecting confidential family and personal information.
6. Establishing long-term succession and continuity arrangements.

The framework does not represent a claim that every proposed scientific hypothesis has been experimentally validated or that every research asset is legally owned by a particular entity.

2. Authorship and contribution transparency

The human originator of the Nexus research program retains recognition for the original research direction, conceptual development, project initiation, and human-authored contributions.

The development process has included substantial AI-assisted collaboration involving:

- Manuscript drafting and restructuring.
- Mathematical analysis and technical examination.
- Programming and computational development.
- Software debugging and testing.
- Research organization and documentation.
- Editorial refinement and critical review.

AI assistance should be disclosed transparently in relevant publication and contribution statements.

AI systems are not represented as human authors, legal persons, or independent owners of intellectual property.

3. Intellectual-property inventory

The project should maintain an inventory of its assets, including:

Literary and research materials

- Original manuscripts.
- Revised manuscripts.
- Research notes and supporting documentation.
- Mathematical derivations and original explanatory material.

Software and computational materials

- Nexus Thermodynamic Laboratory source code.
- Experimental and verification scripts.
- Computational models and simulations.
- Data-processing tools and supporting documentation.

Identity and branding

- Nexus Sovereign name.
- Nexus research-program identity.
- Logos, visual materials, and associated branding.

Technical inventions and confidential information

- Potentially patentable inventions.
- Unpublished technical methods.
- Confidential engineering information.
- Research processes qualifying for trade-secret protection.

Each asset should have a recorded creation history, contributor information, applicable rights, version, and publication status.

4. Publication and licensing policy

The research program intends to support independent scientific examination and responsible public access.

The proposed licensing approach is Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0), subject to verification of ownership and licensing authority.

Third-party material, independently licensed software, external datasets, and other restricted materials must remain subject to their respective rights and permissions.

Publication must not imply ownership of material that the project does not own.

Attribution, version history, and research provenance should be retained.

5. Confidentiality and family privacy

Public research documents should not disclose unnecessary personal information.

The following should be maintained separately from public research releases:

- Legal names and private contact information where disclosure is unnecessary.
- Family and estate-planning records.
- Account credentials and security information.
- Confidential correspondence.
- Private financial and legal documents.
- Unpublished information requiring legitimate confidentiality protection.

Confidentiality provisions should be limited to information that actually qualifies for protection and should not be misrepresented as an openly published research document.

6. Research provenance and integrity

The project should preserve:

- Original and revised manuscript versions.
- SHA-256 checksums.
- Dated source archives.
- Software verification records.
- Experimental datasets and their provenance.
- Negative results and documented limitations.
- Contributor and revision histories.

A checksum establishes file integrity relative to a known reference. It does not independently establish scientific correctness, authorship, or legal ownership.

7. Legal structure and ownership review

Before assigning ownership or licensing rights to the Nexus Sovereign Foundation, its legal status and authority must be verified.

A qualified attorney should review:

- Whether a legal entity exists.
- Its formation and governing documents.
- Ownership of existing intellectual property.
- Any necessary assignments or licenses.
- Trademark availability and registration strategy.
- Patentability and disclosure risks.
- Contractual relationships and third-party rights.

No ownership transfer should be presumed merely because an organizational name appears in a manuscript.

8. Family protection and succession

The project should be incorporated into an appropriate personal estate-planning strategy.

Professional advice should address:

- Wills and revocable living trusts where appropriate.
- Powers of attorney.
- Beneficiary designations.
- Intellectual-property succession.
- Access to essential records.
- Continuity of research operations.
- Designation of trusted individuals authorized to administer project assets.

Family members should not automatically assume personal liability for project obligations merely because they are related to its originator.

9. Governance principles

The Nexus Sovereign research program adopts the following principles:

1. Honest attribution.
2. Evidence-based scientific claims.
3. Transparent AI contribution disclosure.
4. Respect for third-party intellectual property.
5. Responsible public access.
6. Protection of legitimate confidential information.
7. Preservation of research history.
8. Clear separation between scientific evidence and philosophical interpretation.
9. No unsupported claims of legal ownership or scientific validation.
10. Long-term continuity and family privacy.

10. Legal review and implementation

This document is a preliminary governance framework, not a legal instrument, intellectual-property assignment, trust, corporate formation document, or substitute for professional legal advice.

Implementation requires review by qualified United States intellectual-property and California estate-planning counsel.

End of working draft.
